# AgentGraph v0.16.0-rc.2

Status: **Release candidate for integration testing**. Local verification date: 2026-08-31. Stable version 0.15.1 is unchanged.

RC2 adds recovery of committed pending delays to the [RC1 durability hardening](v0.16.0.md). Select `heiner/agent-graph:0.16.0-rc.2` explicitly; existing `^0.15.1` consumers stay on the stable line. This publication does not certify a consuming plugin, host application, or production database.

## What changed after RC1

`AgentGraph::recover($runId)` can resubmit a valid pending delay through the currently bound `DelayScheduler` after queue dispatch fails or an acknowledged job is lost. Recovery preserves the original interrupt ID, absolute due time, checkpoint, saved results, and `delayed` status. It does not replay the waiting node or its observers, resolve the interrupt, or grant approval.

The durable run/checkpoint/interrupt binding and registered graph version must agree. Missing or mismatched authority and noncanonical saved timestamps fail closed. Scheduler errors remain visible while the committed wait stays recoverable. Ordinary input waits and terminal runs are unchanged.

Custom schedulers must tolerate repeated scheduling of the same `(runId, interrupt_id)` without changing delivery authority or extending the due time. This is a clarified behavioral contract, not a new public method signature. RC2 adds no migration beyond RC1's claim-token migration.

The [delay recovery guide](../guides/delay-recovery.md) records the exact contract, fault tests, and the comparison with fixed LangGraph source commit `11ee185999b86bfea2d8c0e69cef9a5e37acf686`. Those references support recovery from durable state; they do not imply that LangGraph core supplies this SDK's Laravel timer transport.

## Upgrade requirements

- From RC1: review custom `DelayScheduler` implementations and overrides of `GraphRuntime::recover()` / `recoverLocked()`, then verify repeated scheduling and real delivery.
- From 0.15.1: also adapt the task-attempt and node-claim-token store contracts, apply the additive claim-token migration, and drain old processes before the coordinated restart. Never mix 0.15 and 0.16 execution against the same runtime records.
- Legacy 0.15.1 delay checkpoints lack the required `runtime.wait` marker. Recovery does not synthesize it; use an explicit host reconciliation procedure.
- Consuming plugins must preserve immutable deployment and graph-version authority. Updating Composer constraints is not permission to rewrite stored artifacts.

See [UPGRADE.md](../../UPGRADE.md) and the [Filament integration guide](../guides/filament-plugin-upgrade-0.16.md). The two-store RC1 patch does not by itself make the plugin scheduler or its accepted-parent-response recovery compatible.

## Remaining limits

- Recovery is explicit; this release does not add background discovery of stranded waits.
- Due-time enforcement belongs to the asynchronous scheduler and transport. The default job does not independently reject early manual invocation. `sync` and `deferred` queues are not durable delayed transports.
- Parent cancellation does not automatically cascade through an arbitrary child tree. Asynchronous subgraph orchestration and the consuming plugin's child-resume authorization are unchanged.
- A remote operation may succeed before its receipt is stored, and expired leases cannot cancel already-running external work. Stable operation identities, provider idempotency where available, and reconciliation of unknown outcomes remain necessary.

These changes address a demonstrated lost-delivery boundary. They do not promise exactly-once external effects, model-independent perfect dialogue, or universal fault-free execution.
